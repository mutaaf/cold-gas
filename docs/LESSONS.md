# Lessons

Mistakes already paid for. Read before working.

- **Convex slices have internal edges.** Rapier resolves a contact by its shortest way out; when a body is pressed a centimetre into one slice, the next slice's end face, standing flush with the surface, is shorter, and the normal comes out horizontal. That is a dead stop at speed. Bury every internal face (`sliceShape` in src/build/objects.js runs each slice on under its neighbour and dips it); don't butt slices end to end.
- **Loft winding.** `loft()` in src/render/cars.js must wind (a, b, c) so normals face out; inverted winding back-face-culls every body and lights the inside of the far wall (looked like black slabs).
- **No MSAA HalfFloat render target.** A multisampled HalfFloat composer target rendered pure black on some GPU paths; we use SMAA after the OutputPass instead.
- **Grounded attitude jets.** The flight computer must not fire on the wheels, or it damps suspension bounce with air and sheds mist on the start line.
- **Level-controller signs.** World-up in body frame: roll correction is `-u.x`, pitch correction is `+u.z`. Both were once flipped and the car tipped itself over on landing.
- **Free air without thrusters.** A tall kicker let a fast car clear the lineup with no thrust at all; the ramp is 1.2 m and no-trick jumps are capped.
- **aurora-field `fromText` threshold is 0–1.** Passing 128 matched no pixels and threw, which stalled the theatre. Every visual step is now wrapped in `safe()`, and a hard deadline guarantees the words land.
- **Fast models outrun the show.** The edge model can finish before the theatre reaches COMPUTE, so tokens are queued and played through COMPUTE at their recorded pace. The counters keep the real timings.
- **Gemma 4 on Workers AI thinks by default.** Without `chat_template_kwargs: { enable_thinking: false }` it spends 800 tokens reasoning and returns no answer within 400.
- **Local e2e in the cloud sandbox (2026-10-20).** `@playwright/test` 1.64 looks for `chromium_headless_shell-1248`, but the sandbox ships `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194`. Don't `playwright install`; run e2e through a throwaway, uncommitted config that spreads `playwright.config.js` and sets `use.launchOptions.executablePath: '/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194/chrome-linux/chrome'`. The full suite then runs locally in about a minute.
- **Engines disagree on transcendental functions.** The same input log scored 77.3 in Node, 76.0 in workerd and 71.3 in production, because Math.sin/atan2/hypot aren't required to be bit-identical and the physics amplified the difference. The fix is `src/sim/dmath.js` (fdlibm kernels using only +, −, ×, ÷ and √). Node, Chromium, WebKit and workerd are now bit-identical.
- **+x is the car's LEFT.** In a right-handed frame with +y up and +z forward, the driver's right is −x. The sim once called +x "right", and steer, roll and yaw all came out mirrored on screen; players called it unusable. Inputs are now driver-relative (+ = right), converted inside `vehicle.js`, and both the unit tests and the e2e tests check that right means right.
- **One fill of nitrogen is one stunt.** After the Wall the tanks are at about a third of their mass, and with blowdown (p ∝ m^1.375) the four lift jets barely match the car's weight. So the Show is one flight, not two stunts chained: the row sits under the ledge, and a ≈0.15 s rear-jet burn (≈20 kg) is the whole cost of carrying over it.
- **A parked car is a body, not a wall.** Each run owns its world (`createWorld`); contact forces go back onto the body they touch (`pushBody`), and stepWorld integrates them against parked-tyre friction. Never mutate the course's static boxes, or one run's shove leaks into the next (and into the server's replay).
- **Full throttle up the curl throws the car off the ledge at ≈24 m/s.** That's real and it's the player's call; the naive-show sweep (scripts/naive-show.mjs) shows the spread.
- **The HTML is served uncompressed.** `no-transform` (which keeps the zone's analytics beacon out) also stops Cloudflare compressing index.html, and the stylesheet is inlined there for first paint. Keep CSS lean, and keep the font faces latin-only. Nothing renders until `stage.ready` (src/render/scene.js), so anything added to the scene after first load is compiled on first sight: warm it with `renderer.compileAsync`.
- **Decals lose to a slope-scaled offset.** The approach strip used `polygonOffsetFactor: -1`; at grazing angles that pulls it toward the camera by more than the 4 mm the paint sat above it, so the paint flickered and mostly vanished. A layer on top must carry a stronger offset than the layer under it (`DECAL` in src/render/sliver.js), not just a lift. Measure flicker with `scripts/flicker.mjs` (heatmaps with `--heat=dir`) rather than by eye.
- **Sub-pixel emissive geometry flickers through the bloom.** The composer's HalfFloat target has no MSAA (see above), so a strip under a pixel wide is sampled on and off and bloom spreads every pop. Give such meshes an `aSpread` attribute and a `sliver()` material. Keep the lights opaque: alpha-blended LED strips measured worse (their blended edges sort against the moving car, mist and plumes).
- **Where a jet sits is how it flies.** Down-jets behind each wheel aren't symmetric about the CoM, so equal valves pitch the car; the mixer's TRIM cancels that and costs lift. The CoM position (an ESTIMATE) now sets the lift budget: source it before tuning landing feel.
- **Touching a spec file puts all of it under the flake guard (2026-10-20).** Changing `tests/e2e/smoke.spec.js` made CI repeat every test in it 5× in parallel. The BYOK test, where each click waits on the title's software-GL frames (2–5 s each), then ran past 60 s on 4 of 5 runs. It's just as slow on main. Slow-but-correct tests get `test.slow()` with a comment saying why. Never drop assertions, and don't move a new test to another file to dodge the guard.
- **Instanced quads need DoubleSide.** The crash sparks are camera-facing streaks built in the vertex shader. Their screen-space winding comes out the same for every spark, so with the default FrontSide every one was culled. The pool was full and nothing showed.
- **A spring is not a crash.** A fully elastic hull spring sank a hard hit 0.3–0.5 m into the ground and fired it back out at e ≈ 0.5–1. Bodywork yields and stays folded (vehicle.js HULL_YIELD). Keep the floor probes compliant: they are the skirts riding the deck, and making them stiff moved the Wall reference off the lip.
- **Anything async that draws over the stage needs an off switch.** The judges' theatre had no abort, so a restart mid-call left it playing over the next run. Long-lived overlays take an AbortSignal, and every flow change (startRun, toTitle) goes through closeOverlays().
- **Integrate angular momentum, not angular velocity.** The explicit ω × Iω gyroscopic term diverges once ω·dt gets large (Pro mode has no rate limit). L += τ·dt in the world frame, ω = I⁻¹·Rᵀ·L is stable at any rate. Fuzz Pro inputs (/tmp-style random tricks, see sim.test 'spin stability') after any integrator change.
- **A naive bot is not a person (2026-10-20).** The naive sweeps hold keys perfectly from the right instant; people react 0.2–0.4 s late, overshoot a held roll by a reaction time, boost until the cue goes away and press LAND when they think of it. `scripts/human-show.mjs` models that, and it found the Show landed 10 % of the time while the naive sweep said 85 %.
- **A sine-law leveller asks for nothing upside down.** Correcting by sin(tilt) gives zero demand at 180°: LAND held while inverted rolled out at ≈0.5 rad/s. Level by the angle, roll-only in the lower hemisphere.
- **Plan a cold-gas brake on the pressure at the END of the stop.** A 15 m/s stop drains ≈70 kg and the regulator loses line pressure below 65 % gas, so the last metre has half the thrust. But a timid plan brakes early, fights gravity longer and drains more: cold gas rewards one late, hard burn.
- **Calibrate a HUD against outcomes.** A predictor that scaled lift by the car's tilt went red during every roll-out and taught early, gas-wasting braking; one that charged the whole stop at the end-of-stop thrust put the BRAKE line 20 m up. Test it: red must mean a hard touchdown, and LAND held must never see red.
- **An even pair of down-jets pitches the car.** The rear jets sit 1.74 m behind the CoM and the fronts 1.15 m ahead: chords (LIFT, a side pair) must open at the pitch-free trim, or every roll tap kicks the nose down.
- **One key, two meanings.** Shift was LAND in the air and BOOST on the ground, so holding LAND through the touchdown fired the rear jets. Context-dependent keys need the context to end where the player expects (Shift boosts only on the run-up now).
- **A fan law fitted to the climb must not starve the launch (2026-10-20).** Scheduling suction from the deck's tilt alone idles the fan on the level, and the 0–60 gate (fan much faster than tyres) failed: the fans also "fill the void" below 50 mph. Keying that to tyre saturation or TC duty chattered (more suction → less slip → less suction) and leaked into the climb fit. Key it to the driver's command (held full throttle, a full stop on the move), filtered so a crawl's throttle blips don't count. Fit the law with `tests/unit/fan.test.js`, not by eye.
- **A traction-control floor is a torque, not a grip (2026-10-20).** 15 % of the command was tuned on concrete; on grass it is more than a rear tyre can carry, so the wheel never comes back to the slip peak and the car slides down a gentle verge with its wheels spinning. Limit torque to what the surface carries (vehicle.js, `loose` materials).
- **The ground beside a road is graded, not natural.** A road conformed only by its structural cut/fill slopes keeps the mountainside right up to its shoulder. A car off the road must be able to stop and drive back: a 1:4 apron, rounded at the hinge. Test it by driving off and back on (`scripts/grass-probe.mjs`), not by looking.
- **Measure a surface by bisection, not phi.** A probe above the road beside a guard rail is nearer the rail than the road; `top − phi` isn't a height there.
- **Free ports aren't free (2026-10-20).** Several agents run `vite preview` at once; with `reuseExistingServer` a busy port silently tests someone else's build. Pick an unusual `E2E_PORT` and check `lsof -i` first.
- **Compare G like the livestream reads it (2026-10-20).** The reveal's G-FORCE channel was read at 2 Hz; a per-tick `maxG` catches contact and valve-opening spikes (the Show's "3.5 g peak" was the release kick at 27.7 m, not the braking). Average specific force over ≥ 0.25 s before comparing it with the telemetry, and say where in the flight the peak happened.
- **One NaN pixel is a flicker storm (2026-10-20).** The searchlight beams faded with `pow(1. - vUv.y, 1.7)`; interpolation can carry v a hair past 1 at the far rim for some sub-pixel camera positions, `pow` of a negative base is NaN, and the bloom smeared that NaN over much of the frame for a frame at a time. It was most of the Show replay's measured flicker (`scripts/flicker.mjs` show-replay: wall 13.0 → 0.4, car 21.6 → 3.1, sky 4.5 → 0.2). Clamp whatever goes into `pow`, `sqrt` or `normalize` if interpolation could push it out of range. A z-fight probe pose flipping exactly 500‰ (half the nudges, the whole frame) is the signature.
- **Lamps in haze: fade the halo up close (2026-10-20).** The atmosphere pass (src/render/film.js) integrates each lamp's in-scatter along the ray. A camera standing inside a halo (the replay rig at the foot of the Wall, metres from the uplights and the red glow) sees it as a veil that swings with every pan: replay sky jitter went 4.5 → 7.2. Halos fade out within a few radii of the camera and fall off with the ray's miss distance, so they don't lift the whole sky.
- **The title wipes the address bar (2026-10-20).** `toTitle()` rewrites the URL to the course, which also dropped a deep link's `#b=` build. Capture anything you need from `location` before the first `toTitle()`, and put a `?drive=` link back (main.js does now).
- **A quest needs a clear field.** Laid over the player's own build, the AI's rings and kickers landed inside their loops. The build steps aside for the quest (kept, not saved over) and comes back after.
- **A rigid contact needs no push on some ticks (2026-10-20).** On the engine (Rapier), a car lying on its roof can be in contact with zero impulse for a tick; "touching = impulse > 0" made the hull flicker in and out of contact and the 120-tick flip counter never filled. Touching is impulse > 0 *or* within 1 cm. And a rigid contact resolves within the tick: telemetry that reads a closing speed (touchdown sink, an impact's speed) must read it from before the step, and against what was hit as it was before the step.
- **Don't teleport a body the engine is holding.** Writing the car's pose into the engine every tick while it rested on a face reset the contact's warm start and it jittered. While in contact the engine's pose is the car's pose: only write it back after free flight.
- **A single-point fan seals on thin air.** The old fan read one gap at the CoM, and the old deck's distance field said "inside" past the lip, so the reference hung with its floor half over the edge at full suction. The seal is eight patches now, each leaking on its own: hang with four wheels and the whole sealed floor on the deck (the Wall reference brakes at 93 %).
- **Bundler-only WASM glue doesn't load everywhere.** Rapier's npm build uses `import * as wasm from './x.wasm'` and extensionless imports: Node, Vitest and workerd can't load it as shipped. `scripts/vendor-rapier.mjs` bundles the JS API once; each host instantiates the untouched .wasm its own way (src/sim/engine.js). workerd may not compile WASM from bytes: the Worker imports the .wasm as a module.
- **A late, hard brake has no slack (2026-10-21, 0013).** When LAND waits in free fall and brakes at 80 % of full lift, three things the old early, gentle brake used to hide all crashed the car. First, steering for the pad during the free fall: tilting does nothing without thrust, and the brake then starts 30° off vertical. Second, the mixer's attitude-first priority, where one pitch correction took half the lift. Third, an altimeter that measured from the ground, so the car met a 4.1 m semi still falling at 15 m/s. Steer only while braking, give the brake priority when it is behind the profile, and read the roofs along the car's whole footprint (the gaps in the row are narrower than the car). Run `naive-show` and `human-show` after any change to the governor's timing.
- **Re-measure a ticket's premise on main before building it (2026-10-21, 0014).** 0014 was filed against a 0.55 m/s set-down that 0013 had already replaced, and one of its bars (Wall ≤ 1.5 s below 3 m) came from a REPORTED line that the TELEMETRY table contradicts (2.0 s). When a ticket's acceptance criteria conflict with each other or with a telemetry-anchored test, sweep the knob, write the numbers into the ticket, and send it back to `proposed`. Don't trade one reveal fact for another.
- **Driving production headless needs `skipCountdown()` (2026-10-21, critic).** On SwiftShader the countdown runs on frame time and `startRun('reveal')` sat in `countdown` for over 30 s. Await `__coldgas.physicsReady`, call `startRun`, then `__coldgas.skipCountdown()`. Measure HUD layout with element bounding boxes, not by eye: the phone readouts sat at x 470–494 on a 390 px screen and looked like a cropped dial.
- **A resting thumb flies the car (2026-10-20).** The touch stick had no dead zone, and in Assist any roll over 0.05 is a rate command that also switches off LAND's hold-the-line. A thumb left on the stick in the air drifts ±0.1. The stick now has a 15 % dead zone; measure phone play with `scripts/human-phone.mjs`, not by eye.
- **Local e2e shares port 4175 with every other worktree (2026-10-20).** `reuseExistingServer` silently ran the suite against another worktree's build and it passed. Run local e2e with a port of your own: `E2E_PORT=4793 npx playwright test`.
- **Headless Chromium has no motion sensor, and its emulated screen is landscape (2026-10-20).** It fires `deviceorientation` with null angles (ignore them), the CDP orientation override doesn't reach the page, and `screen.orientation.angle` is 90 even in a portrait mobile viewport, so tilt reads beta, not gamma. Tests dispatch their own readings and tilt both axes.
- **A frozen sim fades the cue line.** The cue is refreshed every frame while it applies; pause the sim to screenshot and its timer runs out mid-fade. The HUD audit holds it on before measuring contrast.
- **The flake guard can run a PR out of time (2026-10-20).** Changing smoke.spec (9 slow tests) put 55 runs through the guard at 5× and the 25-minute CI job was cancelled with every test green. The guard now budgets repeats by test count (≈40 runs, never under 3× each); it still repeats every changed test with no retries.
- **…and by test length (2026-10-21).** The solid-scenery spec's two tests take ≈3 min each under CI's software GL; at 5× they hit their timeout and the job's 25 minutes. Tag such tests `@heavy` (the guard runs them 2×); the job is now 35 min, since the main suite alone takes ≈18.
- **A scripted drive is chaotic: keep it on dmath, and keep its colliders true (2026-10-21).** The endless e2e drive passed on main by luck. The test pilot steered with Math.atan2/sin, so CI's Chromium drove a different 2 km than Node or a local browser. And the hull's heightfield sampled the road WITH its guard rails: a vertex on a rail took the rail's top and ramped 0.6 m up into the road, which threw the car 6 m in the air at 136 km/h. A physics change (0015's tyre relaxation) moved which side of that coin the run landed on, and CI went red. Sample the ground alone for the hull (rails and roofs are their own boxes), check a sampler converged before trusting it, and put anything that steers a recorded or asserted drive on dmath.
- **Probe what the car asks for, not only what it can do (2026-10-21, critic).** With suction forced on, the tyres held 2.4 g at 30 m/s, but with the fan just armed the car cornered at 1.19 g: the fan schedule had no lateral term and the rack's lock was sized for μg. Measure a claimed capability through the same inputs a player gives (stick, `fan: 1`), then force the subsystem on to see which layer caps it. In the sandbox, `scripts/perf.mjs` runs if its `chromium.launch` gets `executablePath: '/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194/chrome-linux/chrome'` (a throwaway copy, not committed).
- **Average a G window as Δv, not as per-tick magnitudes (2026-10-21, 0018).** The mean of |a| per tick rectifies a rigid contact's tick-to-tick jitter: the HUD read 1.22 G cruising on the level. The magnitude of the window's mean (|Δv/0.5 s + g|) reads 1.000 there and matches `brakePeakG` in free flight to 0.003. Use it for anything shown on the ground.
- **A pilot's time-to-go carries the profile it was written for (2026-10-21, 0014).** The Lineup pilot steered for the mark with `tGo = h/2.5 + 1.5` s, the old hover-and-set-down. Once the kickflip stopped hovering and the mark moved just past the row, it pitched back at full stick the whole descent, tilted the lift off the brake and hovered over the row at −2.5 m/s (4.2 s airtime, worse than before). When a LAND law changes, re-read every pilot that plans against it, and steer only while braking (as the Wall pilot does).
- **Drive it the way a person does before tuning it (2026-10-21, driving feel 2).** The first driving pass tuned keys, ramps and the rack, and the car was still "unusable": a crawl limiter written for the Wall's curl read every 6° road grade as the curl and cut the motors at 32 km/h, and traction control held drive at the tyre's longitudinal peak, which spends all of its grip on the friction ellipse and spins the car out of every bend under full throttle. Neither shows on a flat bench. `scripts/drive-feel.mjs road` drives the real roads with whole key presses and a reaction time; drive the page too (a keyboard driver and a thumb on the stick) and look at it on a phone held upright, where the camera hid the road. A limiter keyed to geometry ("on a slope") must say which course it is for.
- **A peak with a flat top hides where the limit is (2026-10-21, 0020).** 0020 blamed the rack for tyres-only cornering at 1.12 g, because the fronts ran at 6.3° against an 8.4° peak. On this Magic Formula shape 6.3° already gives 99 % of the peak force, and opening the lock made the corner *worse*. Before blaming the layer above the tyre, sweep the knob and compute the axle's own ceiling (μ·Fz per tyre with load transfer and load sensitivity). A car-level "1.26 G" is not the tyre's μ.
- **A drawn thing the physics hasn't got is a ghost, and a solid thing nobody drew is a wall (2026-10-21).** Trees were drawn and not solid; a far-LOD forest drew among the near one; the land was a bilinear patch under a 4 m triangle mesh (2 m apart on steep cells); the graded apron met the land 2.8 m apart, a cliff nobody drew. Place scenery with one pure function the renderer and the physics both call, give every gfx tier the same near set, and audit both ways by probe: drive at what is drawn, and find what the engine holds where nothing is drawn (`tests/unit/scenery.test.js`, `tests/e2e/scenery.spec.js`).
- **Choose a road leg by height, but the place along it by plan (2026-10-21).** `nearestSeg` scored every segment by plan distance plus a height penalty. Above a deep cut that penalty picked the segment whose centreline was nearest the query's height, metres along the road, so the ground under a point depended on how high you asked from (up to 70 m). Pick the leg by height, then the nearest segment of that leg in plan.
- **A hollow mesh is a trap for a body pressed into it (2026-10-21).** A kicker as a trimesh: at 80 km/h its curve bottoms the suspension (4.5 g), the hull sinks a few centimetres into the thin surface, and the nearest face is then the far end's, which stopped the car dead. Give the engine solid convex slices of a ramp, not its skin.

- **Probe a corner where its circle fits (2026-10-21, 0021).** The steady-corner probe turned right from x = 30 on the Lineup. At 2.9 g its circle missed the parked row; at 2.45 g, after load sensitivity widened it, it hit the row, and the probe read 2.08 g with the speed collapsing. Before trusting a number that drops, check that the car's speed held and its path stayed clear (print pos and speed). Circle diameter is 2v²/a: about 80 m at 30 m/s and 2.4 g.
- **A rate that scales with speed must use |v| (2026-10-21, 0021).** The rack's slew time `RACK_T0 + RACK_TV·vFwd` used signed forward speed. Past 20 m/s in reverse it crossed zero, `clamp(x, lo, hi)` got lo > hi, and the steering ran away to 20 rad with the stick centred. A drive test passed only because the spin had not yet turned the car round at its 6 s sample. Any speed-dependent gain must say what reverse does.
- **A body put down touching the ground is awake for two seconds (2026-10-21, real objects).** A new contact wakes a sleeping Rapier body, so every cone streamed in standing on the ground ran awake for its whole sleep timer, and a slalom of 48 paid for 48 awake bodies each time it came into range. Put a resting prop down 5 mm up (`PROP_LIFT`): it stays asleep until something touches it, then drops into place.
- **Stream a knocked body by where it is, not where it was placed (2026-10-21, real objects).** The piece store answers "what is near" from placements, so a cone the car snowploughed 60 m down the apron was released the moment the car passed its starting spot, and the test read NaN from its frozen pose. Keep every active body that is still within reach of where it is now, and remember where a released one was left.
- **Hold WEBGL_lose_context before the loss (2026-10-21, obs).** Once a context is lost, `getExtension()` returns null, so three's `renderer.forceContextRestore()` (which looks the extension up then) can't restore anything and the page stayed black. src/obs/heal.js takes the extension at boot and calls its `restoreContext()`. The first frames after a restore recompile every shader: seconds each on SwiftShader, so e2e waits on frames generously.
- **Check what a ticket says is missing before ranking it (2026-10-22, scout).** 0023 claims the sim has no rolling resistance, but vehicle.js:901 already has a viscous `Fx -= vLong * 6`; the real gap is a load-based Crr term. Grep the code for the premise when grooming, and headless Chromium here needs `--use-angle` dropped from scripts/shots.mjs (the Mac flag hangs the page).
- **`__coldgas.physicsReady` is a function, not a promise (2026-10-21, 0022).** `await window.__coldgas.physicsReady` resolves at once; if the WASM isn't up yet, `startRun` defers to a later task and a test that steps the run in the same `evaluate` samples nothing (1 in 36 under 6 parallel workers). Wait with `page.waitForFunction(() => window.__coldgas.physicsReady())`. And a test that steps the sim itself (`__coldgas.liveStep`) should start the run in the same `evaluate`, or set `S.paused` so the frame loop can't step it unsampled between calls.
- **SMAA is not a cure for shimmer, and the flicker probe's masks must not depend on the canvas (2026-10-21, 0037).** On the phone profile SMAA (even its LOW preset) made sub-pixel edges pop *more* frame to frame (show-wide car/row/wall up); FXAA's sub-pixel blend brought the title, the Lineup's row and the replay's car down instead, but a distant row seen down a dolly still pops more with it (14 → 30‰). And turning canvas MSAA off moved the probe's numbers by itself: its ID pass drew to the canvas, so MSAA-blended IDs at edges rounded to neighbouring regions. The ID pass now has its own single-sampled target; compare before and after with the same probe, not with a ticket's old table. A full mist pool overwriting its oldest *visible* particle is a pop too: wait for it to fade (src/render/plumes.js).
- **A reload is not a heal when the network is the fault (2026-10-21, 0040).** The self-heal reloaded on any failed lazy chunk; on a flaky phone that reloaded into the browser's offline page and the game was gone. Reload only when online, our own index.html answers (`cache: 'no-store'`), and, for a missing chunk, it names a different entry script (src/obs/reload.js). Don't `preventDefault()` `vite:preloadError` to "degrade": the import then *resolves* `undefined` and the caller destructures nothing. And Vite fetches a lazy chunk twice (its `modulepreload` hint, then the import): a test that fails only the first request tests nothing, and a failed hint is the import's fault, reported once. Count reloads in e2e with `page.on('load')`: `framenavigated` also fires on `history.replaceState`, which `toTitle()` does.
