## Why
0211 builds real Blender assets; this puts them where the procedural ones stand, in the exact same modes: the Show's
parked row, the Lineup's, the Sandbox's cones, barriers and parked car in Free Drive, and the iPhone app's stages. Only
the pixels change: every collider stays the sim's own (docs/OBJECTS.md), so `ref-hash`, the vectors and naive-players
are untouched by construction.

## What
Runs on: the operator's Mac to rebuild the assets or the native stages (needs Blender / Chromium); the game itself needs nothing new.

- `src/render/forge-registry.js` (tiny, main bundle): the flag (`?forge=1|0`, default `FORGE_DEFAULT_ON`) and the registry.
- `src/render/forged.js` (a lazy chunk, ≈ 22 kB gzip): GLTFLoader + MeshoptDecoder from three's own node_modules, the
  manifest (`src/render/forge-manifest.json`, GENERATED by `--promote`), each slot as a `THREE.LOD` with the game's own
  materials (src/render/parked/kit.js) by palette name, the baked occlusion as vertex colours; merged geometry for the
  instanced props.
- main.js: fetched after the title is up, at idle, never on a data-saver connection; a failure keeps the procedural row and
  is not a lazy-chunk failure (no heal, no reload). Swapped at the next cut to the title or a countdown, never under a still
  title, in a run, its results or its replay (an explicit `?forge=1` swaps as soon as they land, for review and audits).
- scene.js `lineup()`: a vehicle's group keeps its pose, name and dents; its contents are swapped (`stage.useForged()`), and
  a course built after the load is built with them. crash.js re-reads a swapped car's dentable meshes.
- src/build/render.js: the Sandbox's cone, barrier and parked car draw the forged geometry once it's loaded.
- scripts/native-stage.mjs `--forge=0|1` (default: the web's): the same .glb files, loaded in the exporter's page before the
  course is built, LOD0 only, baked into `parked-<i>.bin` with the same materials; Stage.swift loads them unchanged.
- `tools/forge/shots.mjs`: the same moments, procedural vs forged, at 390×844 and 1280×720.

## Acceptance criteria
- [x] With the flag on, the Show's and the Lineup's rows are the forged models, from our own origin, after the title
      (tests/e2e/forge.spec.js); `?forge=0` fetches nothing and keeps the procedural row
- [x] The sim is untouched: no file under src/sim changes; a reference run over the forged row scores as before
- [x] Flicker: the row's pops fall on every measured view (scripts/flicker.mjs, Metal GPU): desktop show-wide 17.5 → 12.4 ‰,
      lineup-title 138.2 → 89.8 ‰ (budget 104); phone show-wide 16.3 → 10.3 ‰, lineup-title 93.1 → 40.0 ‰ (budget 142)
- [x] Phone frame time (scripts/perf-show.mjs, 4× CPU, DPR 3): no worse; fewer draws per frame (show run 269 → 255, lineup
      title 67 → 62), render CPU p50 equal or lower
- [x] First load unchanged: the chunk and the models load after the title (≈ 1.1 MB of meshes, lazily)
- [x] The native stages carry the forged row (show, lineup), regenerated by `node scripts/native-stage.mjs show lineup`
- [x] Works at 390×844 touch and 1280×720 keyboard (tools/forge/shots.mjs)

## Test plan
`tests/e2e/forge.spec.js`: the models are requested from `/coldgas/forge/*.glb` on localhost after the title, swapped at the
next cut, every vehicle in the row; a reference run plays and judges over them; `?forge=0` fetches none.
`tests/unit/forge.test.js`: every manifest entry's file exists at its size, is meshopt-compressed, has its LODs, palette
materials, no images, within its slot's budgets and download cap. Manual: flicker and perf-show A/B above.

## Out of scope
- New props a car can reach that the sim doesn't simulate (a support truck beside the pad): a drawn thing the physics
  hasn't got is a ghost (docs/OBJECTS.md), so a new prop needs its collider first.
- Free Drive's endless world (trees, rocks): another agent is on native Free Drive; the forge can fill those slots next.
