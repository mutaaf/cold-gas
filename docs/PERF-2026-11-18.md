# iPhone performance, 2026-11-18

The owner asked for the app to be "100x more performant", measurably. This page is the record: how it's measured, the
baseline on main (feb7cc08), what each PR changed, and the result with all of them. Tickets 0296–0300.

## How it's measured (0296)

`-perf <s>` runs the real game, with its UI, its sim and its HUD, not the bench's tool panel. It waits `-perf-wait <s>`
after the first frame, measures for `<s>` seconds, prints one `PERF` line and quits. `make perf` runs every look × scene on
the "ColdGas iPhone" simulator in Release. `make device-bench-all` runs the same on a phone. `scripts/perf-table.py` turns
the lines into the tables below (`--base` adds the change).

| column | what it is |
|---|---|
| CPU p50/p95/p99 | the main thread's own CPU time (thread clock) in the display link's whole callback: the game's frame, the sim's ticks, the encode |
| encode p50 | the renderer's part of that |
| main ms/frame | the main thread's busy CPU time a frame, SwiftUI's layout and commits included (a run-loop observer) |
| draws, tris, passes | per frame, counted where they're encoded (`RenderStats`) |
| mem / peak MB | phys_footprint, averaged over the window, and the process's peak |
| Metal MB | the GPU memory every kept renderer holds: meshes, textures, targets, shadow map, rings (from each resource's shape, since the simulator reports no size) |
| launch→frame | process start to the first frame drawn (wall clock) |
| Hz asked, fps | the rate the display link was asked for, and what it delivered |

Scenes: `title` (the Show's loop), `show`/`wall`/`lineup` (an autopilot run), `roam` (Roam on the Show), `freedrive` (the
pilot 3 km out), `cycle` (Free Drive in and out three times, then the title), `sheet` (Settings open over the title),
`paused` (a run paused). Looks: Real, Anime, 8-bit.

**Read with care.** This Mac was shared by many sessions while it was measured (load average 300–450). Wall-clock numbers
(launch, fps) swing with that; the CPU columns are thread time, which it barely touches. Draws, triangles, passes, Metal MB
and Hz are counts and don't move with load at all. The simulator's display tops out at 60 Hz and its GPU says nothing
about a phone's, so GPU time is for a phone run only.

What the table says, in order of size:

1. **Frames nobody needed (#447).** Under a sheet or a pause the stage draws 15 frames a second instead of 60 here (a
   ProMotion phone's 120): 75–88 % fewer frames, each a full shadow, MSAA main pass, bloom and film. The title and the
   results ask for 60, not 120 (the simulator's display is 60 Hz, so this one shows on a phone only).
2. **Graphics memory (#448 with #446).** Every renderer kept its full-screen targets and its own 2048² shadow map while
   another course was drawn, and Free Drive loaded a second copy of the Show's set. After three trips into Free Drive the
   title held 179 MB of Metal resources; now 73 (−59 %). Free Drive and Roam: 135 → 70 MB (−48 %); a course's own
   renderer: 86 → 66 MB (−23 %), from the 16-bit shadow map. On a phone #446 also drops MetalFX's 12.6 MB output texture.
3. **Draw calls (#446).** 13–34 % fewer on every scene and look: the shadow pass draws each mesh once (its casters' indices
   merged) instead of once per submesh, and parked cars outside the key light's box aren't drawn into it.
4. **Launch (#450).** Process start to the first frame, the title, five launches each: baseline 2974, 1781, 2269, 2945,
   3789 ms (median 2945); after 1451, 1736, 1306, 1704, 3191 ms (median 1704, −42 %). Wall clock on a loaded Mac, so the
   spread is wide; every launch but one after is under the baseline's fastest.
5. **CPU a frame.** The main thread's own time falls 5–14 % on the Real and Anime runs, and the renderer's encode 2–25 %;
   Roam and Free Drive's encode drops further where the shadow pass had the most to merge. Three 8-bit cells read slower after (Lineup, Roam and Free Drive, +34 to +109 % CPU) and stand out of line: their
   *baseline* readings are what's off (the 8-bit Lineup encoded 194 draws in 0.29 ms while the 8-bit title took 0.64 ms for
   202 and the Real Lineup 0.73 for 201), and after, they sit with the rest (0.58–0.91 ms). The single-launch matrix ran
   on a Mac at a load average of 300+; a repeat of those cells (3 launches each) was cut short by other sessions on the
   shared simulator, so they're reported as measured and not counted as a win or a loss. A phone run settles them.

Passes are the same on the simulator (13 Real/Anime, 11 on the Lineup, 4 in 8-bit): the pass #446 removes is the
present copy after MetalFX, and the simulator has no MetalFX. On a phone, Real and Anime go from 14 passes to 13.

## What's left (measured, not done)

- **Free Drive's world.** 330 MB of footprint in Roam and Free Drive (the title: 37) and 1.8–1.9 M triangles a frame:
  the terrain, the roads and the props, and #365 (Free Drive, MetalFX/MSAA governor) is rewriting that code. A cap on the
  tile cache and a farther LOD step are the next memory and GPU wins there.
- **The stage's fragment shader** loops over every light for every fragment, 4× MSAA; per-draw light lists, half
  precision in the BRDF and fewer taps in the shadow would be the GPU wins on a phone. They need `make device-bench-all`
  to judge, since the simulator's GPU says nothing.
- **Bloom** is 7 passes (a bright pass, five halvings, a compose); folding the compose into the film pass would save one
  pass and one target, with a small change in the look to check.
- **The encode** is now ≈ 0.6–0.8 ms on the simulator, mostly fixed per-pass cost; reusing render pass descriptors and
  a persistent state cache across draws would trim it further once #420, #429, #434 and #435 (which all add draws to the
  same function) have landed.
- **Instruments** (Time Profiler) wasn't run under the lock in time for this page; `-perf`'s `main ms/frame` is the
  main thread's measure here.
